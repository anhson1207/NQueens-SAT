\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

Several factors condition the interpretation of our findings. First, while standard N-Queens on an empty board admits constructive $O(N)$ solutions, we employ it strictly as a canonical benchmark for comparing how general-purpose reasoning architectures formulate, propagate, and resolve expanding symmetric constraints. These results should not be conflated with the NP-complete N-Queens Completion problem \citep{gent2017complexity}. Second, to establish an un-tuned algorithmic baseline, all Boolean SAT encodings were executed under Glucose3's default phase-saving policy without dynamic random seed manipulation; modifying branching heuristics could radically alter search trajectories. Thus, the timeouts observed in SAT-Product and SAT-Sequential reflect specific heuristic-encoding interactions under this baseline rather than absolute intractability. Third, while our primary metric (pipeline total time) aggregates CNF construction, solver loading, search, and validation, formula generation overhead differs markedly across encodings: Pairwise incurs $\Theta(N^3)$ clauses across the board, Binary requires $\Theta(N^2 \log N)$ total clauses, whereas Sequential, Commander ($k=3$), and Product generate $\Theta(N^2)$ total clauses. Consequently, SAT approaches carry formulation overheads absent in native CP models, though hybrid engines like OR-Tools CP-SAT internally generate Boolean conflict clauses via Lazy Clause Generation. Furthermore, memory consumption was measured as sampled process-tree Resident Set Size (RSS) in MiB, which is subject to sampling discretization (potentially missing inter-sample spikes) and may double-count pages shared across parent and child processes.

The supplementary large-scale experiment at $N=200$ introduces additional validity considerations. Execution was limited to five isolated repetitions per method under a deterministic setting, which measures environment and system runtime variance but does not capture the full distribution of randomized search trajectories. Crucially, the exclusion of SAT-Pairwise at $N=200$ was enforced proactively by a preflight safety policy. Specifically, the preflight protocol established an effective available memory cap of $798.34$~MiB (dynamically computed from host free memory minus a safety reserve) bounded by a maximum policy ceiling of $4,096$~MiB. The analytical structural projection for SAT-Pairwise at $N=200$ was $4,175.18$~MiB (for over $13.2$ million clauses), thereby exceeding both the active headroom cap and the policy ceiling, triggering proactive exclusion (\texttt{NOT\_RUN\_RESOURCE\_POLICY}) to preserve host system stability rather than an unmonitored runtime crash. Similarly, the IBM CP Optimizer's \texttt{LICENSE\_ERROR} was triggered by a commercial Community Edition search-space bound ($2^{1000}$) rather than algorithmic exhaustion. Finally, evaluating a supplementary point at $N=200$ demonstrates extreme-scale feasibility but does not replace continuous interpolation across intermediate problem sizes.

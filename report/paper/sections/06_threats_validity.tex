\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

Several factors condition the interpretation of our results. First, to establish an un-tuned algorithmic baseline, all Boolean SAT encodings were executed under the Glucose3 default phase policy. Modifying branching heuristics could radically alter search trajectories; therefore, the timeouts observed in the SAT-Product encoding may be an artifact of this specific heuristic interaction rather than an inherent failure of the encoding space. Second, the primary metric (pipeline total time) aggregates CNF construction ($O(N^2)$) and search, meaning SAT algorithms are burdened by structural initialization penalties not experienced by $O(N)$ CP architectures. Third, the benchmark metadata indicated minor provenance flags (\texttt{git\_worktree\_dirty} and legacy records), though data structures were strictly validated.
Externally, the study exclusively evaluates the N-Queens problem on a single macOS ARM64 environment up to $N=100$. Extrapolating asymptotic algorithmic complexities beyond this bound or to other combinatorial families (e.g., planning) remains speculative. Finally, the \texttt{BLOCKED\_LICENSE} limits of MIP solvers denote commercial API boundaries, not mathematical execution failures.

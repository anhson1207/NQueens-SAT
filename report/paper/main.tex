\title [mode = title]{A Reproducible Empirical Comparison of SAT Encodings and Exact Solvers for the N-Queens Problem}                      

\author[]{Nguyen Anh Son}
\affiliation[]{organization={University of Engineering and Technology, Vietnam National University, Hanoi}, op={}}

\begin{abstract}
The N-Queens problem is a classic combinatorial benchmark widely used to evaluate algorithmic efficiency in artificial intelligence. This study presents a rigorous empirical comparison of exact solving paradigms---Boolean Satisfiability (SAT), Constraint Programming (CP), and Mixed Integer Programming (MIP). We evaluate five Boolean SAT encodings alongside four exact solvers within a strict, subprocess-isolated, deterministically seeded benchmarking framework. Our primary full benchmark covers up to $N=100$ (495 records), supplemented by a large-scale experiment at $N=200$. 

Our results demonstrate that structural compactness in SAT encodings is a poor predictor of Conflict-Driven Clause Learning (CDCL) resolution time; the SAT-Sequential encoding generated the second-fewest clauses but timed out at $N=200$, whereas heavily parameterized SAT formulations resolved much faster. At $N=200$, SAT-Commander and SAT-Binary successfully completed the benchmark within a second, whereas SAT-Pairwise was excluded due to hardware memory limits ($>4$GB for $13.2$ million clauses). Notably, the SAT-Product encoding exhibited extreme non-monotonic search pathology, timing out at intermediate problem sizes while resolving $N=100$ and $N=200$ successfully. Across all paradigms, native Constraint Programming implementations demonstrated superior memory efficiency by avoiding explicit Boolean expansion; however, they fundamentally remain subject to exponential search spaces. At $N=200$, IBM CP Optimizer's Community Edition hit a hard search-space license limit ($2^{1000}$), emphasizing that both algorithmic complexity and software environment restrictions dictate practical large-scale solvability. This paper provides a fully reproducible dataset and quantitative insights into the practical trade-offs of constraint modeling techniques.
\end{abstract}

\shortauthors{N.A. Son}

\title [mode = title]{A Reproducible Empirical Comparison of SAT Encodings and Exact Solvers for the N-Queens Problem}                      

\author[]{Nguyen Anh Son}
\affiliation[]{organization={University of Engineering and Technology, Vietnam National University, Hanoi}, op={}}

\begin{abstract}
The N-Queens problem is a classic combinatorial benchmark widely used to evaluate algorithmic efficiency in artificial intelligence. This study presents a rigorous empirical comparison of exact solving paradigms---Boolean Satisfiability (SAT), Constraint Programming (CP), and Mixed Integer Programming (MIP). We evaluate five Boolean SAT encodings alongside four exact solvers within a strict, subprocess-isolated, deterministically seeded benchmarking framework. Our primary full benchmark covers up to $N=100$ (495 planned official trials), supplemented by a large-scale experiment at $N=200$ (45 planned trials). 

Our results demonstrate that structural compactness in SAT encodings is a poor predictor of Conflict-Driven Clause Learning (CDCL) resolution time; the SAT-Sequential encoding generated the second-fewest clauses at $N=100$ but timed out at $N=200$, whereas heavily parameterized SAT formulations resolved much faster. At $N=200$, among the four configurations that solved successfully, SAT-Commander achieved the lowest median pipeline runtime ($0.441$~s) and lowest peak sampled process-tree RSS ($146.2$~MiB), whereas SAT-Pairwise was proactively excluded by a preflight memory policy ($>4$~GiB projected for $13.2$ million clauses vs.\ $798.34$~MiB available cap). Notably, the SAT-Product encoding exhibited extreme non-monotonic search pathology, timing out at intermediate problem sizes ($N \in \{40, 44, 50, 64\}$) while resolving $N=100$ and $N=200$ successfully. Constraint Programming offers a compact high-level formulation using $N$ integer decision variables and global constraints, avoiding the need to explicitly construct the SAT CNF encodings evaluated in this study. However, the observed memory consumption depends on the solver implementation and does not consistently favor CP across all tested configurations. At $N=200$, IBM CP Optimizer's Community Edition hit a hard search-space license limit ($2^{1000}$), emphasizing that both algorithmic complexity and software environment restrictions dictate practical large-scale solvability. This paper provides a fully reproducible dataset and quantitative insights into the practical trade-offs of constraint modeling techniques.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data Availability}
The complete source code, solver implementations, test suites, and benchmarking reproduction scripts are openly available in the GitHub repository: \url{https://github.com/anhson1207/NQueens-SAT}. All experimental datasets (including raw benchmark JSONL logs, processed CSV matrices, environment metadata, and statistical figures) are tracked directly within the repository under the \path{results/} directory:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Primary Full Benchmark ($N \le 100$):} Contains 513 records total (495 planned official trials and 18 warmup runs) across 9 solving methods at \path{results/raw/benchmark_nqueens_primary_full.jsonl}, with processed summaries and environment telemetry at \path{results/metadata/}.
    \item \textbf{Supplementary Large-Scale Experiment ($N=200$):} Contains 45 scheduled trials (20 SAT, 5 TIMEOUT, 5 LICENSE\_ERROR, 10 BLOCKED\_LICENSE, 5 NOT\_RUN\_RESOURCE\_POLICY) recorded at \path{results/raw/benchmark_nqueens_large_scale_n200.jsonl}, accompanied by preflight environment telemetry and cryptographic source tree fingerprints at \path{results/metadata/benchmark_nqueens_large_scale_n200_preflight.json}.
    \item \textbf{Historical Provenance:} Earlier development logs include 269 legacy trial records gathered prior to schema fingerprint automation, which were independently validated via configuration and outcome consistency without retroactive hash alteration.
\end{itemize}

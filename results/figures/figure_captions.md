# Figure Captions

**Figure 1**: Median pipeline total time for five SAT At-Most-One encodings across various board sizes (N), bounded by a 300-second internal timeout. Runtimes denote end-to-end processing—including encoding, solving, and validation—collected exclusively from successful iterations among the five independent repetitions per size. A uniform `solver_default` phase policy was applied to the Glucose3 SAT solver. Missing data points indicate zero successful runs at that N.

**Figure 2**: Median end-to-end runtime progression of four exact solving approaches (OR-Tools CP-SAT, Gurobi MIP, IBM CPLEX MIP, and IBM CP Optimizer) across different problem sizes. Metrics are derived from valid, completed runs over five independent repetitions per size. Execution of MIP solvers (Gurobi and CPLEX) is subject to community license boundaries, thus lacking data points beyond their respective size limitations (N=44 and N=31).

**Figure 3**: A comparative summary of the median pipeline total times across all nine solving methods at a uniform board size of N=20, a scale resolvable by all solvers within respective license limits. Values represent the median of five independent repetitions. Methods are categorized by their solving paradigm (SAT, CP, MIP), and failures or unmet execution conditions are annotated explicitly.

**Figure 4**: The growth trajectory of CNF clause counts corresponding to five distinct SAT At-Most-One encodings as the board size (N) increases. Data is directly measured from the encoding module output prior to solving, highlighting the asymptotic structural complexity of each logic formulation.

**Figure 5**: Breakdown of boolean variables generated by five SAT encodings at N=100. The stacked bars separate primary decision variables (fixed at N² = 10,000) from auxiliary variables introduced to enforce constraints. Values denote empirical counts recorded during model construction.

**Figure 6**: A matrix visualizing the execution status and reliability of nine solving methods across all evaluated board sizes. Cells indicate the fraction of successful runs out of five repetitions. Distinct markers explicitly identify total timeouts, errors, and omissions strictly stemming from license constraints, clarifying apparent performance gaps in exact solvers.

**Figure 7**: Distribution and variability of pipeline total runtimes for successful trials over five independent repetitions, highlighted at representative board sizes (N = 20, 32, 50, 100). The visualization employs a logarithmic scale, presenting only solvers with successful completions to illustrate stability across stochastic executions.
